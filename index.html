About

After studying at Paris IV–Sorbonne and St Andrews, Simon Gabay began writing his doctoral dissertation in Latin philology at the University of Amsterdam (UvA) on the history of the actor in the Middle Ages. For his postdoctoral work, he turned his research toward computational philology within the framework of a Swiss National Science Foundation (SNSF) project on Sévigné’s manuscripts at the University of Neuchâtel, where he created the first courses in digital humanities.

In 2020, he joined the University of Geneva as an assistant professor (Maître-assistant) in the chair of Béatrice Joyeux-Prunel, where he leads the FreEM project (resources and applications for Classical French) and FoNDUE (information extraction from historical documents). Previously, he directed the Katabase project (study of the manuscript market at the end of the 19th century) and Galli(corpor)a (development of tools for restructuring historical documents from Gallica).

His main research areas are early modern French philology, history of philology, classical literature, natural language processing, and optical character recognition.

His publications that are freely accessible online are available on HAL.
